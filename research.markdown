## Upstream Overlap and Downstream Airline Competition

I examine how common regional subcontractors influence competition among U.S. airlines. Using domestic ticket data from 1998–2016, I document that carriers sharing independent regional operators exhibit closer fare movements and smaller fare gaps. A repeated pricing model shows how more informative public signals under common subcontracting can sustain cooperative outcomes. I estimate demand, costs, and conduct jointly, allowing cooperation to vary with subcontracting links. The estimates associate greater common subcontracting with stronger internalization of rivals’ profits and attribute much of the regional fare premium to markups. These findings highlight how vertical relationships can shape competition through connections spanning multiple markets.
